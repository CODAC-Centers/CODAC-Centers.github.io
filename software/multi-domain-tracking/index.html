Multi-Domain Tracking

A cellular sheaf over a graph assigns a vector space to every vertex and edge and a linear map to every incidence. A global section is an assignment of a vector to each vertex that every restriction map agrees on — and the space of them, $H^0(X; \mathcal{F})$, is what this widget computes and draws.

The example below is multi-domain tracking. Ships carry positions in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and targets carry positions in $\mathbb{R}^3$, linked by the projection that forgets altitude. Drag an agent; the formation follows the constraints. Pin the ships and four degrees of freedom survive — one altitude per target, which no ship can observe. Pin the targets instead and none do. That count is the DOF remaining readout, and it is $\dim H^0$ for the pinned set.

What the controls do

  • Drag an agent to move it. Pinned agents are the boundary condition; the rest relax to the nearest global section.
  • Diffusion time scrubs the sheaf Laplacian’s heat flow: 0 is the agents as placed, $\infty$ is the section they relax to.
  • Flex n of k steps through a basis of $\ker L_{ff}$ — the deformations the pins leave unconstrained — naming the coordinates each one moves, and Sweep animates the selected one. A null mode costs no Dirichlet energy by construction: it is a direction no edge can see.
  • Presentation mode scales every font, stroke and marker up for a projector.

The kernel basis is sparsified before it is shown. An eigendecomposition returns an arbitrary orthonormal basis, and an arbitrary basis of a space with sparse members is dense — every sweep would move every agent at once. Column operations that strictly reduce support are kept, which leaves the span untouched and turns the tracking sheaf’s seven-dimensional kernel into the readable one: four single-target altitudes, two planar translations, and a rotation.

Other sheaves

This page is one scenario, fixed. The Cellular Sheaf Explorer is the same engine with the other examples, a source editor for writing your own sheaf, and the analytics panels.

Relationship to CellularSheaves.jl

The widget implements the same construction as CellularSheaves.jl in the AlgebraicJulia ecosystem, in the browser and with no server. It runs entirely client-side: nothing you drag leaves the page.
