Six center papers accepted across CDC, ACC, ECC, IFAC and Asilomar

Six papers from the center have been accepted this year, spanning the thrusts. The full list, including work already published, is on the publications page.

Conference on Decision and Control

Two papers appear at the 65th IEEE Conference on Decision and Control.

From Local to Global: Sheaf-Theoretic Control Barrier Functions on Manifolds. Keith A. Currier, Wilmer Leal, Brandon C. Fallin, James Fairbanks and Warren Dixon.

Quantale-Enriched Co-Design: Toward a Framework for Quantitative Heterogeneous System Design. Hans Riess and Matthew Hale at Georgia Tech, Yujun Huang and Gioele Zardini at MIT, and Matthew Klawonn at the Air Force Research Laboratory. Monotone co-design models components as feasibility relations between required resources and provided functionalities, but its boolean formulation cannot natively carry quantitative criteria such as cost or confidence. In practice those are added afterwards by scalarization, which obscures the structure of the system. This invited paper lifts co-design from boolean feasibility to quantitative evaluation by modeling resources and functionalities as quantale-enriched categories, and shows that series, parallel and feedback composition all survive the move. Change-of-base maps between quantales then let different subsystems be evaluated in different local semantics and still composed in one framework. arXiv:2603.29921

American Control Conference

Asynchronous Nonlinear Sheaf Diffusion for Multi-Agent Coordination. Y. Zhao, Tyler Hanks, Hans Riess, Samuel Cohen, Matthew Hale and James Fairbanks. Coordination sheaves model heterogeneous agents and their goals over a communication topology, and sheaf diffusion drives the agents to meet those goals. The existing theory assumes agents communicate and compute in lockstep, an assumption that delays and uneven hardware break. This paper drops that assumption: under bounded delays, nonlinear sheaf diffusion still converges to a minimizer of the coordination sheaf’s Dirichlet energy, from arbitrary initial conditions, at a linear rate set by the delay bound. arXiv:2510.00270

European Control Conference

Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Multi-Target Tracking using Cellular Sheaves. Tyler Hanks, Cristian F. Nino, Joana Bou Barcelo, Austin Copeland, Warren Dixon and James Fairbanks. Graph Laplacian methods, the usual tool here, do not handle agents whose state spaces differ in dimension. This paper models them natively with cellular sheaves. Coordination sheaves have until now been aimed at cooperative problems like consensus, and this work extends them to the non-cooperative problem of tracking several unknown targets, posed as a harmonic extension problem. The decentralized control law built from the sheaf Laplacian comes with a Lyapunov-based proof that the tracking error converges. arXiv:2512.24886

IFAC

Whitney Control Barrier Functions: A Mesh-based Geometric Approach via Discrete Exterior Calculus. Keith Currier, Wilmer Leal, George Rauta, Austin Copeland, Warren Dixon and James Fairbanks.

Asilomar

Multi-Agent System Identification with Nonlinear Sheaf Diffusion. Nivar Anwer, Hans Riess and Matthew Hale, at the Asilomar Conference on Signals, Systems, and Computers. Trajectory data records only how node states evolve, and so exposes nothing beyond the aggregate effect of the edge forces at each node. Distinct interaction laws that agree node-wise cannot be told apart from trajectories alone. The paper shows the obstruction to recovering the true law is topological, measured by sheaf cohomology, and that unique recovery from an unconstrained function class is possible exactly when that cohomology vanishes. A cautionary result falls out of it: reproducing trajectories accurately does not certify that the underlying interaction law has been recovered. arXiv:2605.11204
